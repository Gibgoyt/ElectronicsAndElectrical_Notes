# Coulomb's law

Electric charge from first principles, and everything that follows from the force between two
charges: current as charge per second, ![1 A = 1 C times s^-1](README.assets/eq-inline/1ac70fbaf0.svg)<!--m:1\ \mathrm{A} = 1\ \mathrm{C\cdot s^{-1}}-->, Coulomb's law
![F = |q_1 q_2|/(4 pi epsilon_0 r^2)](README.assets/eq-inline/4f27242d6a.svg)<!--m:F = |q_1 q_2|/(4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2)-->, superposition, the electric field, the volt as
![1 J times C^-1](README.assets/eq-inline/fbd2f47b0d.svg)<!--m:1\ \mathrm{J\cdot C^{-1}}-->, Gauss's law, and the parallel-plate capacitance
![C = epsilon_0 A/d](README.assets/eq-inline/f699fc1a96.svg)<!--m:C = \varepsilon_0 A/d-->. The full argument is in [coulombs-law.md](coulombs-law.md), in twelve
sections with nine figures (Figures 91–99), three of them animated.

> **The thesis in one line**
>
> Like charges repel and unlike charges attract, with a force proportional to each charge and to the
> inverse square of their distance. The field, the volt, Gauss's law and the capacitor all follow
> from that one law.

## Contents

The treatment covers:

1. Electric charge — two kinds, quantised in units of ![e = 1.602 times 10^-19 C](README.assets/eq-inline/e82ad9559e.svg)<!--m:e = 1.602\times10^{-19}\ \mathrm{C}-->, and
   conserved.
2. Current is charge in motion — ![I = dQ/dt](README.assets/eq-inline/0f3a69426a.svg)<!--m:I = dQ/dt-->, electrons per second, and the drift speed in copper
   (Figure 91, animated).
3. Coulomb's law with every symbol and unit, including ![epsilon_0](README.assets/eq-inline/961a0cda39.svg)<!--m:\varepsilon_0--> in
   ![C^2 times N^-1 times m^-2](README.assets/eq-inline/20823b6560.svg)<!--m:\mathrm{C^{2}\cdot N^{-1}\cdot m^{-2}}--> and why the ![4 pi](README.assets/eq-inline/63f4e29993.svg)<!--m:4\pi--> is there (Figures 92, animated, and 93).
4. Worked numbers: two electrons, the hydrogen atom, and 1 C against 1 C at 1 m, which is about
   ![9 times 10^9 N](README.assets/eq-inline/c55f07652d.svg)<!--m:9\times10^9\ \mathrm{N}-->.
5. The vector form and superposition, with a worked three-charge example (Figure 94).
6. The electric field ![E = F/q_0](README.assets/eq-inline/526cb315cd.svg)<!--m:\vec E = \vec F/q_0-->, the field of a point charge, and field lines (Figures
   95 and 96, animated).
7. Work, potential and voltage: ![V = W/q](README.assets/eq-inline/5f3ac748e3.svg)<!--m:V = W/q-->, the uniform field ![E = V/d](README.assets/eq-inline/14bd374217.svg)<!--m:E = V/d-->, the point-charge potential
   ![kq/r](README.assets/eq-inline/49644ded37.svg)<!--m:kq/r-->, and why voltage depends only on the end points.
8. Gauss's law, derived from Coulomb's law: why ![1/r^2](README.assets/eq-inline/d790d0aaad.svg)<!--m:1/r^2--> gives the same flux through every sphere
   (Figure 97).
9. Parallel plates: the uniform field, ![C = epsilon_0 A/d](README.assets/eq-inline/f699fc1a96.svg)<!--m:C = \varepsilon_0 A/d-->, ![epsilon_0](README.assets/eq-inline/961a0cda39.svg)<!--m:\varepsilon_0--> in
   ![F times m^-1](README.assets/eq-inline/8fd760381c.svg)<!--m:\mathrm{F\cdot m^{-1}}-->, and the energy density ![1 over 2 epsilon_0 E^2](README.assets/eq-inline/34d16350a6.svg)<!--m:\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 E^2--> (Figure 98).
10. Why there is no magnetic charge, which leads into Ampère's law (Figure 99).
11. What this costs you — the limits of the law, breakdown, and why you cannot hold a coulomb.
12. Sources and cross-links.
